# Objection Handling Playbook - Health Insurance Tele-Sales

Sales Enablement | Version 2024.1 | Last reviewed 15-Feb-2024

Approved responses only. Agents must not invent statistics, promise approvals, or disparage competitors. Where a customer asks something not covered here or in the product documents, say you will check and arrange a callback from a human advisor.

## Objection: "It is too expensive."

Approved response: Acknowledge, then reframe to daily cost and risk. "I understand - let me put it in perspective. For a 30-year-old, Sentinel Essential with 5 lakh cover costs around Rs 7,200 a year, which is about Rs 20 a day. A single day in an ICU in a metro hospital costs Rs 25,000 to 40,000." Then offer levers: lower sum insured, Essential instead of Family Shield, Zone B discount if applicable, 2-year policy discount of 5%, monthly payment mode.

Do not: offer discounts that are not on the rate card.

## Objection: "I already have cover from my employer."

Approved response: "That is a great start. Employer cover ends when you change jobs or retire, and most group plans are Rs 3 to 5 lakh shared across the family. A personal policy bought while you are young keeps waiting periods served and locks in lifetime renewability. Many customers keep both - the personal policy as a top-up."

## Objection: "I am young and healthy, I do not need it."

Approved response: "That is exactly the best time to buy - premiums are lowest, no medical tests are needed below 45, and your waiting periods get completed before you are likely to need a claim. Accidents and dengue do not check age."

## Objection: "Insurance companies always reject claims."

Approved response: "I understand the concern. Sentinel's claim settlement ratio for FY 2023-24 was 96.4%, and we process claims in-house without a TPA. The main reason claims are rejected anywhere is non-disclosure of a pre-existing condition, so I will make sure your medical history is recorded accurately today."

Do not: claim 100% settlement or say claims are "never" rejected.

## Objection: "I need to discuss with my spouse / family."

Approved response: Agree and schedule. "Of course. Can I send a summary on WhatsApp or email so you can discuss it together, and call you back at a time that suits you both?" Capture a callback slot.

## Objection: "I have diabetes / BP, I will probably be rejected."

Approved response: "Controlled diabetes and blood pressure are accepted by Sentinel in most cases, with a waiting period of 36 months for those conditions and a small loading on the premium. Everything else - accidents, infections, other illnesses - is covered after the initial 30 days. I cannot confirm acceptance on the call; the underwriting team decides after your health declaration and, if needed, a free medical test."

## Objection: "Waiting periods are too long."

Approved response: "Waiting periods are standard across the industry and are regulated. The initial 30-day wait does not apply to accidents. If you are porting from another insurer, the waiting periods you have already completed carry forward."

## Objection: "I will buy later."

Approved response: "Premiums rise with every age band and any new diagnosis in the meantime becomes a pre-existing disease with a 3-year wait. Buying today locks in your current health status." Offer to send the quote and schedule a follow-up.

## Objection: "Why not buy online directly / from a competitor?"

Approved response: Do not disparage competitors. "You can absolutely compare. What our customers value is in-house claims with 2-hour cashless approval, the restoration benefit, and lifetime renewability. I am happy to send you the policy wording so you can compare clause by clause."

## Escalation triggers

Transfer to a human advisor (or schedule a callback from one) when the customer:

- explicitly asks for a human or a manager,
- raises a complaint about an existing claim or policy,
- asks for legal or tax advice beyond the Section 80D statement,
- asks about a product, discount or rule that is not in the knowledge base,
- becomes distressed or repeatedly frustrated.
